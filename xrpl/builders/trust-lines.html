Before you start

Use the exact asset identity from lesson 1. A trust line belongs to the classic fungible-token model; this is not an NFT purchase or native XRP setup. No signing is needed for this worksheet.

Your outcome: Read balance, limit, issuer controls and reserve implications without confusing a settings change with receiving money.

Words you will use

Trust line
A ledger relationship for a currency between two accounts. It does not certify trustworthiness.
Limit
A receiving limit in that relationship, not a payment or a guarantee against loss.
Balance
The amount recorded from the account perspective being shown. Check which side an explorer is displaying.
Reserve
XRP that must remain available to support an account and relevant ledger objects; it is different from a transaction fee.
Rippling
Moving balances through compatible trust-line relationships to complete a payment. No Ripple changes which paths may be used.

Understand what changes

TrustSet creates or modifies a trust-line relationship. Its LimitAmount identifies the currency, the counterparty issuer and the limit. Native XRP does not use trust lines. This lesson covers trust-line tokens, not MPT authorization.

Read limit and balance separately

Suppose a fictional holder has a limit of 100 units and a balance of 25. The limit is not a payment of 100, and the holder has not received 100 tokens. Limits govern the trust relationship; explicit trading can produce balances above a limit. Do not treat the number as an all-purpose loss cap.

Understand the controls

Authorization may be required by an issuer before a holder can hold its currency. Freeze settings can restrict the asset. Rippling can move value across compatible trust-line relationships as part of payments. No Ripple settings affect those paths; they do not make a token safe or remove issuer risk. Review both account and line settings before changing defaults.

Plan for reserves and removal

Ledger objects can affect the XRP reserve needed by an account. Reserve rules and amounts can change, and trust-line exceptions exist: check the current network rules rather than assuming every line costs the same. Setting a limit to zero does not erase a nonzero balance or guarantee immediate removal. A removable line must meet the ledger’s default-state conditions.

Read a member account before changing anything

Your paper account belongs to Member B. It holds 25 units of the club credit from Issuer A, with a receiving limit of 100. No new payment has been made. Use only these fictional facts; do not manufacture a real issuer address.

  1. Draw four separate boxes labeled network, currency plus issuer, balance and limit. Put Testnet, club credit/Issuer A, 25 and 100 into them. Explain aloud: changing the limit to 200 would not make the balance 200.
  2. Add a control column. Note whether issuer authorization is required and granted, whether freeze restrictions exist, and what the relevant ripple settings are. Unknown is a valid result: it means you need evidence, not a guessed default.
  3. Model an ordinary additional receipt of 30 units with permissions and limits otherwise satisfied. The illustrative balance becomes 55, not 130. Then model a proposed ordinary receipt of 60 more: it would exceed the 100 receiving limit. Do not generalize this exercise to every trading path, where limits behave differently.
  4. Plan reserve inspection separately from the token quantity. Obtain the network current base/incremental reserves and the account relevant owner objects when implementing a real tool. Do not multiply every visible token balance by one fixed XRP charge: trust-line and other object rules have exceptions.
  5. Write a departure plan: resolve the held balance through a deliberate redemption or transfer decision, then inspect non-default line settings on both sides. Setting the limit to zero alone is not proof the line disappeared. There can still be a balance or other state keeping it present.
  6. Ask whether the task needs a trust line at all. Reading a public record needs no signature. Receiving native XRP needs no trust line. Avoid adding a setting just because an advertisement labels it mandatory.
Worked decision

A support message says your 25 credits are stuck because your limit is only 100 and asks you to sign an unlimited limit. That explanation does not follow from the sample. Inspect the exact failure, issuer/network, authorization, freezes, destination and intended operation first. Increasing a limit is not a general repair for token problems.

Optional: connect the worksheet to a public record

In a read-only explorer account view, open the issued balances or trust-line section. Locate the precise currency and peer issuer, then compare balance, limit and any shown freeze/authorization/ripple fields. Identify whose perspective is displayed. If a field is missing from the interface, write not shown rather than assuming disabled. Your learning worksheet can stay fictional if no suitable record is available.

Common problems and what to check

An explorer displays a negative balance

Check whose perspective it uses and the two accounts in the line. A raw shared ledger entry is not always displayed from the holder perspective. Do not infer a debt from a sign without that context.

There is XRP but a new object cannot be created

Total balance is not the same as XRP available above reserve and fee needs. Check the exact result and current reserve rules before changing unrelated settings.

The line remains after the limit is zero

Inspect remaining balance and non-default state. Do not repeatedly submit the same setting or use an unknown token-clearing service.

Before you act

Never change rippling, freeze or authorization flags just to clear a warning you do not understand. A successful TrustSet is a settings change, not a purchase or a safety endorsement.

Check your understanding

Do you need a trust line to receive native XRP?

Will setting the limit to zero erase tokens already held?

Balance is 25; limit changes from 100 to 200. What is the new balance before any payment?

An explorer asks for a signature just to inspect a public trust line. Is that necessary for reading?
