Fake Issuers

Token name alone means nothing. Bad actors can use the same ticker and trick people who only look at symbol and logo.

  • Always verify issuer
  • Do not trust ticker alone
  • Do not trust logo alone

Blind Signing

If you do not understand what you are signing, do not sign it. This is basic survival.

  • Read what the wallet is asking you to approve
  • Verify destination, issuer, and asset
  • Urgency is a red flag

OTC Risk

Direct deals remove platform protection. That means the trust problem lands directly on you.

  • Higher counterparty risk
  • No marketplace buffer
  • Good for private deals only when both sides are real and verified

Exchange Risk

Exchanges are useful. They are also company platforms. That means platform outages, account restrictions, and custody risk are real.

  • Good for on-ramp and off-ramp
  • Bad place to forget long-term self-custody
  • Not the same thing as wallet ownership

Seed Protection

Your seed, secret, recovery phrase, or private key never gets shared. Ever. Anyone asking for it is either ignorant or trying to take your assets.

Reality

Most losses are not magic hacks. They are bad verification, bad trust, blind approval, weak custody habits, or people being lazy with details.

How People Actually Get Burned

Most people don’t lose money because of advanced attacks. They lose money because they trust the wrong person, rush decisions, or don’t verify what they are doing.

  • They follow a loud account that has never used the ledger
  • They trust a token because of the name and logo
  • They sign something without reading it
  • They send funds OTC to someone they don’t know
  • They leave assets on an exchange and forget who controls it

Who To Avoid

  • Anyone rushing you into a decision
  • Anyone avoiding showing issuer or wallet details
  • Anyone saying “just sign it”
  • Anyone using pressure instead of clarity

Real operators show you everything. Bad actors remove time and visibility.
